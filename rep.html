// Lesson 1 · Prospecting

How to Find Unlimited Free Leads

You never need to pay for a lead. Every business in your city is a prospect, and most have a website that's costing them customers. Here's how to find them — free — and walk in with proof they need you.

1. Friends & associates who own a business

Start with your warm network — the fastest yeses you'll ever get. List everyone you know who owns or runs a business. Then look each one up and audit them before you reach out:

  • Pull up their website on your phone. Time how long it takes to load — over 3 seconds is a problem.
  • Run an error check: broken links, no mobile version, no click-to-call, contact form that doesn't work, expired SSL ("Not secure" in the address bar), outdated info.
  • Google their business name + "reviews" and check their Google Business Profile — missing photos, few reviews, no website link = easy wins.
  • Free audit tools: PageSpeed Insights (speed), Google's Mobile-Friendly check, and the free Website Audit Checklist PDF on our site.

Then reach out with a specific observation, not a pitch: "Hey — was on your site and noticed it's not loading the contact button on mobile. That's costing you calls. Want me to fix it?"

2. Google Maps cold calling

This is an unlimited well. Open Google Maps and search a niche + city, e.g. "attorney in New York," "roofer in Mesa," "dentist in Scottsdale." You get a list of dozens of businesses with phone numbers.

  • Click each one. No website listed? Perfect — they need everything.
  • Has a website? Open it and audit it live (see step 1) so you have a reason to call.
  • Use the portal Dialer to call straight down the list. Add each conversation to your pipeline.
  • Opener: "Hi, I help [niche] in [city] get more customers from their website — I noticed yours [specific issue]. Got 60 seconds?"

3. Google Business Profiles with no website

On Maps, filter for businesses whose profile has a phone but no website link. They're already paying for visibility and have nowhere to send people. Highest-intent, lowest-effort close.

4. Facebook groups & Nextdoor

Local "[City] small business owners" groups and Nextdoor recommendations are full of owners. Watch for "anyone know a good web person?" posts — and for businesses posting with no link in their bio.

5. Networking: BNI, Chamber, meetups

One BNI or Chamber of Commerce meeting is a room full of business owners who refer each other. Become the "web/marketing person" in two or three groups and referrals never stop.

6. Drive-by & service trucks

Bandit signs, truck wraps, and storefronts are advertised phone numbers. Snap a photo, look them up that night, audit, and call. Local + visible = they're spending on marketing already.

7. Bad reviews & old sites on review platforms

Yelp, Angi, and Google listings with dated or broken sites are prospects who already invest in being found. A quick audit gives you a reason to reach out with value first.

The system

  1. Build a list (network → Maps niche/city → no-website profiles).
  2. Audit before you reach out — speed, mobile, click-to-call, SSL, reviews.
  3. Lead with the problem you found, not the pitch.
  4. Dial the list from the portal and log every deal in your pipeline.

Do 20 audited calls a day and you'll never have an empty pipeline. Pro tip: send the free Website Audit Checklist as your follow-up — it does the selling for you.

// Playbook · v1

The Field Playbook

This is the only doc you need to start closing. Read it once, keep it open on calls.

1. Who we are

John Bert Web Strategy builds custom websites, apps, and ad campaigns for local businesses in the Phoenix area, and runs the marketing engine on top — AI voice & chat agents, lead & visitor tracking, email/SMS, reputation, and buy-intent data. We deliver fast (5–7 days for sites) and we're a real human who picks up. That's the wedge.

2. Who to sell to

Local service businesses that live or die on the phone ringing: home services (HVAC, plumbing, roofing, landscaping), contractors, clinics, restaurants, gyms, med-spas. If they're spending on ads or have an ugly/slow site, they're a fit.

3. The offer & pricing

  • Website — $294/mo (hosting, edits, support) or $3,500 one-time.
  • App development — from $5,000, scoped per project.
  • Ad campaigns — $100/wk + 10% of spend, 3-month min, money-back guarantee.
  • AI Voice Agent — from $497/mo. Answers & books every call, 24/7.
  • AI Chat & SMS — from $297/mo. Speed-to-lead + missed-call text-back.
  • Buy-Intent Data — from $697/mo. Know who's in-market before they call.

4. Discovery questions

  1. "How are customers finding you right now?"
  2. "When a call comes in after hours, what happens to it?"
  3. "What's a new customer worth to you?"
  4. "If we filled your calendar without you lifting a finger, what would that be worth?"

5. The pitch (30 seconds)

"Most local businesses lose customers to a slow website and missed calls. We fix both — a custom site in about a week, plus an AI that answers every call and texts every lead back in seconds. You only pay for what works, and there's a money-back guarantee on ads. Want me to show you what it'd look like for your business?"

6. Objection handling

  • "Too expensive." → "One saved job a month covers it. The voice agent alone catches calls you're losing today."
  • "I already have a website." → "Great — is it bringing you calls? Let's audit it free and see where it's leaking customers."
  • "I need to think about it." → "Totally fair. What's the one thing you'd need to see to feel confident?"
  • "I'm too busy." → "That's exactly why this exists — we do all of it, you just answer the booked jobs."

7. How to submit a deal

Go to Pipeline & Deals → Enter a deal. Add the contact, business, service, and value, set the stage, and hit Add deal. Keep it updated as it moves — that's how you get paid.

8. How you get paid

See Commissions for the full structure: 15% on one-time sites, first month + 5% residual on monthly services, 20% + setup share on high-ticket. Quarterly bonus at 10 closed deals.
